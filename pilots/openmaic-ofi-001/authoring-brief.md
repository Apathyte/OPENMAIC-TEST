```text
Create a 25–35 minute interactive vocational workshop for two adult learners who are becoming digital/operational champions in an industrial manufacturing plant.

Working title: Where Did This Number Come From?

Learning outcome:
By the end, each learner can trace an OEE claim through the actual production and information chain, distinguish evidence from assumptions, challenge an unsupported AI or Digital Twin claim constructively, and define the smallest credible evidence-gathering experiment.

Sanitised case context:
- Management sees OEE of approximately 70% and expects 80%.
- Variable raw-material quality may affect throughput, yield, colour, and other outcomes.
- ABB 800xA and local PLCs contain process/control information.
- A legacy production tool is still involved in some batchmaking activity.
- BrightEye Momentum MES supports orders/reporting and sends information toward SAP.
- LabVantage contains laboratory results.
- Excel is used and Power BI is planned.
- There is no plant historian or PI system providing a ready-made time-series evidence trail.

Treat every item above as case context, not proof of a working interface or validated lineage. Do not invent tag names, batch IDs, sample links, integrations, timestamps, calculations, or system capabilities. Explicitly label unknowns and evidence that would need to be checked on site.

Teaching style:
- Workshop/vocational hybrid, not a generic Industry 4.0 lecture.
- Plain language and short screens.
- Learners must make decisions, ask questions, and produce an output.
- Include realistic disagreement and organisational pressure.
- Give learners language they can actually use on the shopfloor.
- Treat operator knowledge as evidence to examine, not folklore to dismiss.
- Show where official process descriptions and actual work may diverge.

Use these role perspectives:
1. Experienced batchmaker/operator.
2. Automation engineer.
3. MES/IT specialist.
4. Laboratory/quality specialist.
5. Manager asking why the number is not 80% and proposing AI or a Digital Twin.

Build six scenes:
1. Hook: the 70% versus 80% challenge and the proposed technology answer.
2. Interactive mapping: learner traces material, batch, event, lab, MES, and ERP evidence.
3. Evidence gap: learner identifies what cannot be proven without timestamps, lineage, or a historian.
4. GO/STOP decision: learner decides whether the AI/Digital Twin claim is ready and explains why.
5. Shopfloor rehearsal: a multi-role meeting where the learner practises firm, non-dismissive questions.
6. PBL finish: learner defines the smallest credible experiment/data request, its owner, evidence, time window, and completion criteria.

Include at least these usable learner phrases:
- “Which event, batch, timestamp, and source system support that number?”
- “What do we know, what are we assuming, and what can we test?”
- “How is the lab sample linked back to the exact production conditions?”
- “What is the smallest experiment that would change this decision?”

End with a one-page learner output titled Evidence Request / Smallest Credible Experiment. It must contain:
- operational question;
- current claim;
- known evidence and source;
- missing evidence;
- suspected lineage break;
- proposed collection or experiment;
- owner;
- time window;
- success/stop criteria;
- next decision enabled.

Avoid grading learners on jargon. Grade the quality of their evidence chain, questions, and proposed next action.
```
